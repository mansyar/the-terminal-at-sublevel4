# Product Guidelines: The Terminal at Sub-Level 4

## Voice & Tone (In-Game Writing)

- **System text:** clinical, terse, procedural. All-caps identifiers, timestamps to the second, no personality — until the anomaly learns to imitate it. The system voice's *lack* of personality is a character.
- **Human text (NPCs):** distinct voices — Arisova (urgent, commanding, increasingly unravelling), Miller (military clipped, deflects questions), Chen (practical, numbers-first, nervous when power is involved). Panic is expressed through *degraded protocol*, not exclamation marks.
- **Lore files:** cold bureaucratic language hiding horror between the lines. The dread is in what directives *don't* say.
- **Never:** purple prose, jump-scare text, meta-commentary, or humor that punctures tension.

## UX Principles

1. **The terminal is the world.** No menus, dialogs, or overlays that break the diegesis. Every interaction — including deaths and endings — renders as terminal output.
2. **The game never cheats.** All decisive truths are queryable *before* they matter. A lie is always provable in retrospect with information the player had access to. (Fair-play mystery rule — this is non-negotiable.)
3. **Dread via absence, not presence.** Silence in the chat window, a bio-count that stops updating, a door that stops reporting. What stops responding is scarier than what attacks.
4. **Information pacing:** the player can always gather more data; the cost is the clock. Never hard-gate information behind timers shorter than a careful cross-reference.
5. **Typing feel:** every keystroke responds (key SFX, cursor blink); input latency is a design crime in a terminal game.
6. **Accessibility within diegesis:** autocomplete and command history are diegetic terminal features, not UI concessions. Text size and contrast options allowed; color palette stays monochrome (green default, amber option).

## Atmosphere Dos / Don'ts

- **DO:** let the CRT degrade when systems fail (flicker, scanline tear, static bursts) as the *primary* feedback channel for dread.
- **DO:** use the clock. Timestamps drifting, logs arriving out of order, "last seen" fields that stop updating.
- **DON'T:** show the monster. No visuals of the entity, ever — only its effects on data and people.
- **DON'T:** punish curiosity. Querying more should always yield something, even if it's just texture.
